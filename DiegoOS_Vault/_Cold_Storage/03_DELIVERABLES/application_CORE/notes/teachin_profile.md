# CORE Econ — Teaching Profile

## Core Teaching Claim

My teaching profile is grounded in a structured, evidence-based pedagogical approach that combines analytical rigor with sustained student support.

I do not understand teaching as the simple transmission of concepts. I approach it as a process of training students to build clear, evidence-based arguments that connect economic outcomes to institutional design, historical change, and power relations.

For the CORE Econ application, the central teaching claim is:

> I teach economics as a problem-based, evidence-oriented discipline in which students learn to connect theory, data, institutions, and public purpose.

## Teaching Trajectory

My teaching experience spans more than fifteen years, from 2009 to 2025, across several institutional settings:

- UMass Amherst
- UMass University Without Walls
- Wesleyan University
- Amherst College
- Universidad de Santiago de Chile
- Universidad de Chile

Across this trajectory, my responsibilities have developed from discussion and lab leadership to lecturer, instructor of record, and professor.

This experience gives me a broad teaching profile across introductory economics, political economy, macroeconomics, public policy, applied methods, and graduate research design.

## Pedagogical Commitments

## 1. Structured Analytical Progression

I design courses around stable weekly rhythms, short writing cycles, and transparent rubrics.

The objective is to make analytical development visible and cumulative. Students should not encounter critical thinking only in a final paper or high-stakes exam. They should practice it repeatedly, in smaller and more structured forms.

Example:

In Introduction to Political Economy, I used seven short analytical papers and discussion posts to make critical thinking a continuous habit rather than a single high-stakes performance.

This structure helped students learn how to identify an argument, reconstruct evidence, compare interpretations, and formulate their own position with greater precision.

## 2. Argument Under Constraint

I teach students that economic reasoning requires more than correct terminology.

Students need to learn how to connect theory to evidence under real constraints: limited data, competing explanations, institutional complexity, and historical specificity.

For this reason, my assessments reward:

- analytical coherence
- disciplined integration of concepts
- evidence-based inference
- clarity of argument
- awareness of limits

Essay-based exams with prompts provided in advance are one example of this approach. They allow students to prepare seriously while still requiring them to organize, defend, and apply economic arguments.

## 3. High-Support Rigor

I treat rigor as a design problem.

Demanding intellectual work is only fair when it is paired with visible scaffolding, low-stakes practice spaces, and reliable feedback.

My goal is not to reduce difficulty. My goal is to make the path into difficult material more legible.

This is especially important in heterogeneous classrooms, where students often enter with different levels of preparation, confidence, writing experience, and familiarity with economic language.

## Mentorship and Supervision

I approach mentorship as a scaled extension of course design.

Good advising requires:

- early topic definition
- staged milestones
- clear expectations
- predictable feedback intervals
- support in moving from broad interests to researchable questions

I have supervised undergraduate and master’s projects, including M.A. Public Policy students at Universidad de Santiago de Chile working on complex empirical topics such as:

- public procurement governance
- municipal corruption
- feminist political economy

In these settings, I help students move from broad concerns to researchable questions, from questions to evidence strategies, and from evidence to coherent written arguments.

## Teaching Evaluations

My teaching evaluations consistently emphasize:

- organization
- clarity
- fairness
- reliable feedback
- technical support
- dialogic supervision

The evaluations also show a period of intentional redesign during the transition to online and accelerated formats between 2021 and 2023.

By 2024, my course architecture had consolidated into a more stable model, reflected in strong evaluations, including ratings around 4.5 out of 5 at Universidad de Santiago de Chile.

My mentoring evaluations have been especially strong, averaging between 6.8 and 7.0 on a 7-point scale.

## Pedagogical Innovation

My teaching practice includes a strong commitment to pedagogical innovation.

I designed a course that combined prompt engineering with the political economy of digital capitalism. This allowed students to examine generative AI both as a practical tool and as an object of economic analysis.

This course is especially relevant for the CORE Econ application because it shows my capacity to connect:

- economics education
- digital learning tools
- AI literacy
- political economy
- contemporary technological change
- student-facing skill development

## AI Literacy and Responsible Use

I integrate explicit ethical guidelines for AI-assisted writing into my syllabi.

My approach is not to prohibit these tools. Instead, I train students in:

- AI literacy
- transparency
- verification
- responsible use
- source checking
- revision discipline
- intellectual accountability

For me, economics education must help students understand the world they inhabit, including the digital infrastructures that increasingly shape work, knowledge, public reasoning, and economic life.
