## Pedagogical Profile 

My pedagogy is grounded in structured analytical progression, argument under constraint, and high-support rigor. I teach economics as a problem-based and evidence-oriented discipline, where students learn to connect theory, data, institutions, historical change, and public purpose. My courses are designed around stable weekly rhythms, short writing cycles, transparent rubrics, and staged feedback, so that analytical development becomes cumulative rather than concentrated in a single high-stakes assignment. I treat rigor as a design problem: demanding intellectual work should be paired with visible scaffolding, low-stakes practice, and reliable feedback. This approach is especially important in heterogeneous classrooms, where students arrive with different levels of preparation, confidence, and familiarity with economic language. More recently, I have extended this pedagogy into AI literacy and digital learning, training students to use generative tools with transparency, verification, and intellectual accountability.
