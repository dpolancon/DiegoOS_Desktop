
# Strategic Design for CORE Econ Postdoc Application

This document outlines the overarching strategy for your application to the CORE Econ Postdoctoral Research Fellow position. It details how each component of your application package will be articulated to present a coherent and compelling case for your candidacy, emphasizing your unique strengths in AI-enhanced pedagogy, rigorous research on inequality, and global outreach.

## Overarching Strategic Pillars

Your application will be built upon three interconnected pillars:

1.  **AI-Enhanced, Inclusive Economics Education:** Position yourself as a leader in leveraging AI to make economics accessible, rigorous, and engaging for diverse learners, directly aligning with CORE's mission and the specific requirement for developing AI tools.
2.  **Bridging Research and Practice through Structured Pedagogy:** Demonstrate how your rigorous academic research on inequality and your structured, evidence-based teaching methods (especially "High-Support Rigor" and "Scaled Extension of Course Design") prepare students for real-world policy application.
3.  **Global Reach and Multilingual Contribution:** Highlight your linguistic skills and experience in international academic contexts as assets for CORE's global outreach and adoption efforts.

## Articulation Across Application Materials

Each required document will serve to reinforce these strategic pillars.

### 1. Cover Letter

*   **Role:** Executive summary of your application's strategic coherence.
*   **Manifestation:**
    *   Introduce the core narrative, emphasizing your unique blend of AI-pedagogy, research expertise in inequality, and proven ability to mentor diverse students.
    *   Explicitly link your skills to CORE's Objectives A (AI tools) and B (Awareness/Adoption), highlighting your multilingual capabilities for global outreach.
    *   Propose your contribution to developing an interactive, AI-assisted Research Design Module for public policy students, as detailed in [[CORE Econ — Research Design and AI Pedagogy Pitc]].
    *   Reference your [[pedagogical_profile]] and [[mentoring_capacities]] to support claims of structured, high-support pedagogy.
    *   Concisely state your alignment with CORE's mission to broaden economics education through problem-based learning, drawing on your experience with [[Econ105_Experience_CoreAlignment]].

### 2. CV (Curriculum Vitae)

*   **Role:** Evidence of your career trajectory and qualifications.
*   **Manifestation:**
    *   **Dissertation:** Prominently display your dissertation title and abstract, emphasizing its focus on inequality and financial fragility.
    *   **Teaching Experience:** Revise the teaching section to highlight:
        *   Extensive experience across introductory economics, political economy, public policy, and research design.
        *   Specific mention of Econ 105 (Introduction to Political Economy) and its alignment with CORE's problem-based approach.
        *   Graduate-level research design instruction for public policy students.
        *   Mentorship of M.A. Public Policy projects (e.g., public procurement, municipal corruption).
        *   Experience with digital pedagogy and AI literacy integration.
    *   **Publications/Working Papers:** Include any work related to inequality, economic development, or pedagogical innovation.
    *   **Skills:** Detail relevant technical skills (e.g., Markdown, Obsidian, AI integration frameworks) and explicitly list language proficiencies (Spanish: Native, English: Fluent, Portuguese: Basic).
    *   **International Experience:** Note any roles or collaborations in international academic settings.

### 3. Research Statement

*   **Role:** Demonstrates your scholarly contribution and alignment with CORE's research focus.
*   **Manifestation:**
    *   Detail how your research (e.g., on distributive cycles, financial fragility, wealth inequality) directly supports the Stone Centre’s mission to make inequality central to economics.
    *   Explain how your research informs your pedagogical approach, particularly in teaching students to connect economic theory to issues of power, institutions, and distribution.
    *   Connect your research interests to the CORE project’s aim of developing empirically grounded and policy-relevant economics education.

### 4. Teaching and Education Philosophy Statement

*   **Role:** Articulates your pedagogical vision and approach.
*   **Manifestation:**
    *   Anchor your philosophy in the three core commitments from [[pedagogical_profile]]: Structured Analytical Progression, Argument Under Constraint, and High-Support Rigor.
    *   Elaborate on your AI-assisted learning framework ("AI Hygiene," "Imagine, Discern, Integrate," "Protected Analytical Space") and its application to teaching economics and research design to heterogeneous students. Draw on insights from [[AI_Skills_DigitalLiteracy]] and [[CORE Econ — Research Design and AI Pedagogy Pitc]].
    *   Discuss your experience teaching Econ 105 and its relevance to CORE's problem-based approach for diverse audiences, referencing [[Econ105_Experience_CoreAlignment]].
    *   Detail your mentorship philosophy ("Scaled Extension of Course Design") and its success in guiding students to advanced degrees and professional roles, referencing [[mentoring_capacities]].
    *   Highlight your capacity for pedagogical innovation, particularly in developing digital and AI-integrated learning tools.

### 5. Writing Sample

*   **Role:** Showcases your research and communication proficiency.
*   **Manifestation:**
    *   Select a piece that demonstrates rigorous, evidence-based research.
    *   Prioritize a sample that clearly communicates complex economic ideas, ideally touching upon themes of inequality, institutions, policy relevance, or pedagogical innovation.
    *   Ensure it reflects your ability to construct a coherent, well-supported argument.

### 6. Three Nominated Referees

*   **Role:** Provide external validation of your capabilities.
*   **Manifestation:**
    *   Nominate individuals who can speak authoritatively to your research excellence (especially in inequality/political economy), your pedagogical innovation (AI, inclusive teaching, research design), and your potential to contribute to a global, interdisciplinary project.
    *   Ensure referees can attest to your mentorship skills and your ability to engage with diverse student populations.
