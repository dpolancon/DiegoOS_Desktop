# Pedagogical Profile: Evidence-Based Economics and Analytical Mentorship

Your teaching profile reflects a highly structured, evidence-based pedagogical approach that pairs analytical rigor with significant student support. You view teaching not merely as reciting concepts, but as training students to build clear, evidence-based arguments that connect economic outcomes to institutional design, historical change, and power relations.

## 1. Teaching Trajectory and Experience
Your instructional experience spans over 15 years (2009–2025) across diverse institutional settings, including UMass Amherst (Main and UWW), Wesleyan University, Amherst College, Universidad de Santiago de Chile (USACH), and Universidad de Chile. Your responsibilities have systematically progressed from Discussion/Lab Leader to Lecturer, Instructor of Record, and Professor.

You have taught a wide array of courses across different levels and modalities:

- **Introductory and Core Economics:** Instructor of record for Intro to Political Economy (Econ 105), Microeconomics (Econ 203), and Macroeconomics (Econ 204).
- **Upper-Level Electives and Applied Macro:** Marxist Political Economy, Generative AI & Digital Capitalism, and Macroeconomics and Conjunctural Analysis (USACH).
- **Seminars:** First-Year Seminars like Globalization and Development, as well as upper-level seminars like Political Economy of Latin American Development at Wesleyan.
- **Public Policy & Research Design:** At the graduate level, you have taught research design to public policy students, explicitly training them to formulate feasible, intellectually grounded research questions and evidence strategies.

## 2. Core Pedagogical Philosophy
Your course design is anchored by three primary commitments:

### Structured Analytical Progression
You build courses around a stable, predictable weekly cadence, utilizing short writing cycles and transparent rubrics. For example, your Econ 105 course relies on seven short analytical papers and discussion posts to make critical thinking a continuous habit rather than a high-stakes performance.

### Argument Under Constraint
You insist that economic reasoning must connect theory to evidence under realistic constraints. Your assessments, like the essay-based exams with prompts provided in advance, reward analytical coherence, disciplined integration, and evidence-based inference.

### High-Support Rigor
You treat rigor as a "design problem," meaning demanding intellectual work must be matched with visible scaffolding, low-stakes practice spaces, and highly reliable feedback.

## 3. Mentorship and Supervision
You treat advising not as an ancillary task, but as a "scaled extension of course design," utilizing early topic definition, staged milestones, and predictable feedback intervals. You have an extensive track record of supervising undergraduate and master's projects. Notably, you have mentored multiple M.A. Public Policy students at USACH on complex empirical topics, including public procurement governance, local municipal corruption, and feminist political economy.

## 4. Evaluations and Innovations
Your teaching evaluations consistently highlight your organization, clarity, and fairness. Quantitative data shows a strong early baseline, followed by a period of intentional redesign and adaptation during the transition to online/accelerated formats (2021-2023). By 2024, your course architecture consolidated, resulting in stabilized, high ratings (e.g., 4.5/5 at USACH). Mentoring evaluations are exceptionally strong, averaging 6.8 to 7.0 on a 7-point scale, with students praising your reliable, technical, and dialogic support.

### Pedagogical Innovation
Your profile shows a strong capacity for pedagogical innovation. You designed a course combining prompt engineering with the political economy of digital capitalism, and you proactively integrate an explicit ethical framework for AI-assisted writing into your syllabi, prioritizing AI literacy, transparency, and verification over prohibition.
