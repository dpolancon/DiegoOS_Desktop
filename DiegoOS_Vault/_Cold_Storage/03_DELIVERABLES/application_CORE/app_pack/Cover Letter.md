Dear CORE Econ and Stone Centre Selection Committee,

I am writing to apply for the CORE Econ / Stone Centre Postdoctoral Research Fellow position in Economics Education and Inequality. I am a Ph.D. Candidate in Economics at the University of Massachusetts Amherst, with defense expected in June 2026. My work has developed at the intersection of political economy, heterodox macroeconomics, empirical methods, and economics education. This fellowship is especially compelling to me because it joins two commitments that have shaped my academic trajectory: research on distribution, accumulation, and uneven capitalist development, and the design of economics education that helps diverse students reason from real-world problems to theory, evidence, and public purpose.

My research examines how distributional conflict, institutions, and external constraints shape capitalist development. My dissertation, *Capacity Utilization and Accumulation Regimes: Essays in the Political Economy of Growth, Distribution, and Core-Periphery Dynamics*, argues that capacity utilization is not a neutral technical baseline, but an institutional settlement formed through labor relations, distributive struggle, and core-periphery constraints. I develop this argument through critical econometric replication, applied econometrics using time series analysis, theoretical formalization, and comprative historical analysis. The broader portfolio connects functional distribution to structural inequality through work on rural–urban transitions, teacher segregation, gender wage gaps, and macro-financial fragility. My current short-appointment post-doctoral studies working on use of urban land extends this line of research by linking distributive cycles, financial fragility, and wealth-inequality tendencies driven by real state sector, urban restructuring, central banking, and financialization in a core-periphery dynamics. My fit with the Stone Centre is therefore substantive: I study inequality not as a residual outcome, but as a force that shapes accumulation, crisis, and the categories through which contemporary societies have understood the economy. 

That research orientation also informs my teaching. Across more than fifteen years of instruction at UMass Amherst, University of Massachusets, Wesleyan University, Amherst College, Universidad de Santiago de Chile, and Universidad de Chile, I have taught introductory economics, political economy, macroeconomics, economic development, Marxian economics, public policy, and graduate research design. My pedagogy rests on structured analytical progression, argument under constraint, and high-support rigor. I use stable course rhythms, short writing cycles, transparent expectations, and repeated feedback so students learn to turn economic concepts into evidence-based arguments. In Econ 105, Introduction to Political Economy, I taught heterogeneous online cohorts through Bowles, Edwards, and Roosevelt’s *Understanding Capitalism*, a text organized around competition, command, and historical change. Its relevance is pedagogical and connected to the roots of the CORE projects in the lane of contributions made by professor Samuel Bowles and his broader efforts for an education in economics that teaches about the real world. Under these experiences, I have  trained students to approach economics through institutions, inequality, power, globalization, structural transformations, and historical change.

The contribution I would be most interested in developing for CORE is an interactive Research Design Module for economics education. CORE’s strength lies in beginning from real economic problems rather than from technique alone. I would extend that strength by helping students move from economic problems to feasible research questions, critical literature synthesis, conceptual frameworks, iterative hypotheses, and evidence strategies. This proposal grows directly from how I have overcome my own struggles in research designe and particularly from my experience teaching graduate research design to M.A. Public Policy students in Chile, where I supervised projects on public procurement governance, municipal corruption, and feminist political economy. In those settings, students often arrived with urgent policy concerns but needed a disciplined pathway for turning those concerns into researchable questions and defensible empirical strategies under constraints to graduate from the program. A CORE module built around those steps could support heterogeneous senior undergraduate and master graduate students while also building resoruces for instructors such as templates, rubrics, staged exercises, and assessment tools.

I would bring to this work a practical approach to digital pedagogy and responsible AI-assisted learning. My experience with Markdown-based knowledge systems, GitHub, reproducible workflows, and structured digital archives has been organized around one purpose: making complex material navigable through reading paths, concept maps, templates, and reusable learning resources. I approach AI in the same spirit. It should support questioning, synthesis, and verification without replacing student judgment. My “Imagine, Discern, Integrate” approach to AI usage to mentor students in their research projects treats AI as a bounded tool subordinated to human sovereignty: students can use it to explore possible frameworks, support literature reviews, develop notes systems, compare explanations, map evidence gaps, translate technical material, and stress-test research questions, but they remain responsible for verification, interpretation, and argument construction. As a native Spanish speaker, fluent English speaker, and basic Portuguese reader/speaker, I could also contribute to CORE’s awareness and adoption work among Spanish-speaking and Latin American institutions, including engagement around translated materials where appropriate.

Given the UCL anchor of the fellowship, I would be especially interested in engaging with the Stone Centre at UCL and the wider Stone Centre network, subject to the relevant visa or contractual arrangements. I would welcome the opportunity to contribute to CORE’s research-led economics education project by combining inequality-oriented political economy, structured pedagogy, and responsible digital curriculum design. Thank you for considering my application.
