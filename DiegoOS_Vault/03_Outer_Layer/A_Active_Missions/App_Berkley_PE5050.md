## Selection Logic

The application should be calibrated around four evaluative dimensions:

- **Interdisciplinary Fit:** The candidate must not sound like a siloed economist or a siloed political scientist. They must sound like an *interdisciplinary political economist* who naturally bridges methods and theories across fields.
    
- **Methodological & Substantive Rigor:** The research must demonstrate top-tier empirical skills (causal inference, historical data) applied to big, substantive questions in PE (inequality, state capacity, development).
    
- **UC Berkeley DEI Integration:** Berkeley evaluates DEI holistically. The candidate must prove they don't just "care" about diversity, but that they actively integrate it into their research design, classroom pedagogy, and professional service.
    
- **Curriculum Utility:** The committee needs to see that the candidate can teach core PE courses *and* fill gaps in their current offerings (e.g., stepping in to teach Economic History, IPE, or Migration).

## Fit Architecture

- **Core fit:** Interdisciplinary political economy, economic history, institutional conflict, and Latin American/Global South development.
    
- **Research bridge:**
    - Frame HPE as the ultimate interdisciplinary field, combining economic theory, political science, and historical methodology.
    - Highlight how studying extractive institutions and political activation in the Global South provides rigorous, empirical insights into core PE questions (inequality, state-building, democratic backsliding).
        
- **Teaching bridge:**
    - Pitch specific courses for curriculum expansion: "Economic History of the Global South," "International Political Economy," or "The Political Economy of Migration and Development."
    - Emphasize a pedagogical approach that uses empirical, real-world case studies (like Chile/LatAm) to teach quantitative and theoretical concepts to undergraduates.
        
- **Inclusion bridge:**
    - *Research:* Connect the study of marginalized populations and colonial legacies in the Global South to a commitment to studying underrepresented groups and democratizing data access (open science).
    - *Teaching:* Highlight the use of diverse case studies in the classroom to make economics relevant to a diverse student body, and active learning strategies that support first-generation students.
    - *Service:* Point to concrete actions: mentoring junior scholars from underrepresented backgrounds, organizing diverse seminar speakers, or public outreach translating academic research for broader audiences.

## Main Risks

- **Risk 1: Sounding too disciplinary (pure Econ or pure PolSci).**
    - _Mitigation:_ Explicitly use the word "interdisciplinary." Highlight co-authorships across fields, teaching in multiple departments, and the blending of economic theory with historical/political context.
        
- **Risk 2: Treating the UC Berkeley DEI requirements as boilerplate.**
    - _Mitigation:_ Do not copy-paste generic diversity statements. Tie the Research DEI directly to the *substance* of the work (studying the Global South/inequality). Tie Teaching DEI to *specific pedagogical tools* used in the classroom. Tie Service DEI to *actual past actions*.
        
- **Risk 3: Writing sample formatting violations.**
    - _Mitigation:_ Strictly enforce the 5,000–15,000 word count. If submitting a dissertation chapter, ensure the mandatory short abstract of the full dissertation is included at the beginning of the document.
        
- **Risk 4: Incomplete application due to missing Authorization Form.**
    - _Mitigation:_ Add the "Authorization to Release Information Form" to the very top of the submission checklist. It is a hard filter for UC Berkeley.
        
- **Risk 5: Reference letters missing the Nov 2 deadline.**
    - _Mitigation:_ AP Recruit can be clunky for referees. Send them the direct link, clear instructions, and the Nov 2 deadline immediately. Follow up 5 days before the deadline.
